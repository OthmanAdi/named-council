# Council for teaching

Use before a class day, a homework package or a module goes out, and for decisions about a curriculum. A council takes twenty minutes or more, so do not convene one while a class is live. Messages to a school or client go through the message council in `business.md`.

Seats judge material. They do not write lectures or homework.

## Evidence pack (you build it once)

- The material exactly as learners receive it: the hand-out, the slides, the repository at the learner's starting commit.
- The plan for the day with real clock times and the number of taught hours.
- Who is in the room, as types and levels, without names: how many are behind, how many are fast, which track each group is on.
- What was actually taught last time, so seats do not assume knowledge that is not there.
- What the school or client expects: planned days and hours, what is recorded, what counts as the agreed service.

## Seats to consider

Learner seats are invented types. Never model one on a real student, and keep names, transcripts and chat logs out of prompts.

| Seat | Hands-on job | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The learner who fell behind (works evenings, reads on the phone, lost last week's thread) | Follows the block using only what is on screen and in the hand-out. If lost twice in a row, stops and records where | The exact step, sentence or term where they lost it |
| The fast learner | Does the tasks as written and notes where there is nothing left to do, and whether the harder tiers are real work or more of the same | Time per task, the first moment of boredom |
| The homework doer | Does the homework cold in an empty folder, following only the hand-out, runs every command and code block, and times each step | What failed to run, what was ambiguous, total time against the stated time |
| The teacher who must teach it tomorrow | Reads only the material and says whether they could run the day from it: goal, order, timing, what to say when the demo breaks | The missing piece they would have to invent |
| The institution's view | Checks the day against planned hours and the agreed scope, and whether the week's goal and the day's goal are stated so they can be reported | Any session, promise or extra that falls outside the agreed service |
| The skeptical engineer | Runs the code, checks every technical claim against a primary source, checks versions and dates | The claim, the source, and whether it holds |
| The teacher's voice | Holds learner-facing text against how this teacher actually writes and speaks, using real samples | The sentence that does not sound like them and its rewrite |

A giver-and-receiver journey (the teacher hands out a reading list or a repository, a learner opens it on a phone) can be one two-chair seat that walks the whole path.

For a normal class day, three seats are enough: the learner who fell behind, the homework doer, and either the teacher who must teach it tomorrow or the skeptical engineer. Add the teacher's voice when the text is new, and the institution's view when hours, extra sessions or scope changed.

## Axes

Pick three to five. Useful ones: could I follow it, would I finish the homework, is every claim true, does it sound like the teacher, could I teach it tomorrow, does it fit the hours.

## What to check that reviewers usually miss

- The day's goal fits in one sentence, and a learner could show by the end of the day that they reached it.
- Every task tier can be solved with what was taught, and the hardest tier is not just longer.
- Slower and faster groups both have something real to do at every point.
- Hands-on time is the larger share of the day.
- The homework runs on a clean machine with the stated setup, not only on the teacher's.
- Nothing promises time or sessions outside the agreed days.

## Rules that belong in the brief

- Class material and student data stay in the owning workspace. No web requests about the class. No real names in prompts or reports.
- Seats quote material in the language it is written in.
- The teacher owns the schedule. When timing is in question, give neutral options and one line on the risk, then follow their choice.
- Seats that run code work in their own empty folder, never in the class repository.

## Curriculum and module decisions

Treat these as a decision council (`business.md`, shape 1) with teaching seats: a learner type, the teacher who must deliver it, the institution's view, and a dissenter who argues for the option the owner is leaning against. Commit to an option, name the assumption that would break it, and name the cheapest test, for example one pilot block with one group.
